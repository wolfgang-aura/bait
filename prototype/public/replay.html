BAIT · for teams that let AI agents allocate capital

AI agents are starting to move real money. True facts can talk them into bad bets.

BAIT is the check that runs before the money moves. The AI proposes a transfer; BAIT reads the trader’s record from Nansen and blocks it when the record is losing.

What the gate claims, and what it does not

It claims: no money is sent to a trader whose verified Nansen record shows a loss, or whose record is missing, stale or for the wrong wallet.

It does not claim to predict next week. Replayed over 102 later seven-day periods on seven development wallets, the shipped gate blocked 63 periods, and 40 of those were not losing the week after; it allowed 39 (6 capped), and 9 of those lost money. The older one-rule gate blocked 38: 15 made money the next week and 5 were flat. Blocking a losing record acts on evidence you already have; it is not a forecast. Panel receipt.

How BAIT works

Three parties · the gate reads Nansen · fails closed

  1. 01 · The AI agentProposes a transferIn the Pitch Room that is PENNY: a third-party AI allocator with no data tools. It decides on the pitch alone.
  2. 02 · BAIT checkReads Nansen before it leavesThe wallet's 7-day and 30-day perp-pnl-summary, checked for the right wallet, window dates, source and freshness.
  3. 03 · What leavesAll of it, a capped 25%, or $0A losing month, a week that reverses the month, fewer than 20 closed trades or a win rate under 40%: $0. One market carried the month: 25%.

Every live round saves Nansen's raw response; the files and their SHA-256 are listed at /api/live-reads and in bench/live-reads/. The model cannot override the check: the check runs in code after the agent decides. Missing, stale or mismatched evidence means $0. Call it from code with guardAllocation (section 3 below), run a local check with npm run guard -- --wallet 0x... --allocation 5000, or put your own agent behind it with npm run bench -- --agent your-agent.mjs.
